## What to say, and where it goes

**Something in the catalogue is yours and should not be there.** Say so and it
will be dealt with rather than argued about — whether or not you contributed it,
and whether or not the licence would technically permit it. This is the one that
gets answered first.

**Your account or your data.** Withdrawal, removal, or a correction to what is
recorded about you. Contributions are stored in a graph of their own per person,
so erasing someone is a deletion of those graphs rather than a search through
everything. The [contributor terms](/terms) set out what is kept.

**The crawler.** How it identifies itself, how fast it goes, and how to make it
stop are on the [crawler page](/about/crawler), which is what its user agent
points at. If it has been a nuisance, that is worth an email rather than a
`robots.txt` rule — the rule will be honoured, but it will not tell us what went
wrong.

**A source that should be harvested, or should not.** Adding one means reviewing
its terms first, which is a real piece of work rather than a switch. Saying a
source's terms have been misread is very welcome.

**Access in a shape that is not offered.** There are
[several ways in](/services) and none of them needs an account or a key. If none
of them suits, ask — a bulk consumer paginating the SPARQL endpoint costs this
server far more than answering the email would.

## What this is not

There is no support contract and no response-time promise. This is one person
and one small server. Anything about data removal or something being wrongly
published gets looked at quickly; a feature request may sit for a while.
